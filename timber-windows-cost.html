What Affects the Price?

Window Size

The biggest factor. A small bathroom sash (600 × 900mm) costs roughly half of a large reception room window (1200 × 1800mm). Our configurator adjusts the price as you change dimensions.

Glazing Type

Double glazing is the most popular and cost-effective option (U-value 1.4 W/m²K). Triple glazing adds approximately 20–25% to the price but significantly improves thermal and acoustic performance (U-value 1.2 W/m²K). Passive glass is our highest specification at U-value 0.8 W/m²K, ideal for projects aiming for ultra-low energy performance.

Colour & Finish

Single colour finishes are included in the base price — choose from any RAL colour or the full Farrow & Ball palette at no extra cost. Dual colour (different inside and outside) carries a 15% surcharge to cover the additional spray-finishing process.

Window Type

Sash windows are generally more expensive than casement windows of the same size because of the sliding mechanism, pulleys or spring balances, and the more complex joinery involved. Arched windows carry a premium due to the laminated curved timber sections required.

Georgian Bars

Glazing bars add a modest cost per bar. Our configurator lets you see exactly how different bar patterns look in 3D before you commit.

Timber vs uPVC — Long-Term Cost

Timber windows cost more upfront than uPVC. That is a fact. But the total cost over the life of a building tells a different story.

Factor Timber uPVC
Upfront cost (per window) £1,400 – £2,500 £400 – £800
Lifespan 60 – 100 years 20 – 25 years
Maintenance Repaint every 8–12 years None (but cannot be repaired)
50-year cost (10 windows) £16,000 – £28,000 £12,000 – £24,000 (replaced twice)
Conservation area suitable Yes Rarely accepted
Property value impact Adds value Can reduce value on period homes

Over 50 years, the cost difference narrows considerably — and timber adds value to a property while uPVC often reduces it, particularly on period homes in London.

Installation Costs

Installation is quoted separately because it depends on access, scaffolding requirements, the condition of existing openings, and whether structural work is needed. As a rough guide, installation typically adds 30–50% to the supply cost. We provide a firm installation quote after the site survey — no hidden extras.
